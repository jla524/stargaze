An edge processor, a storage payload, a single GPU in orbit, and a license for a million satellites are easy to mix together. Each row here is one system. A company can have several rows.

The board was checked against the [news log](/news/) through 25 November 2026. A date in the milestone column is the date in that statement, not a forecast. The record is the news post that row uses. A later funding story does not move a row unless the status itself changed. Marketplaces, price forecasts, and commentary stay in the news log.

- **Flying.** Hardware this site records as in orbit.
- **Manifested.** Assigned to a named mission. That is not confirmation the spacecraft arrived, and it is not a license.
- **Announced.** A company statement, including a target year. Not a manifest slot and not an application.
- **Authorized.** A regulator's grant to operate, on the conditions in the order. Not a launch license, and not a satellite in orbit.
- **Filed.** An application with a regulator. Not permission to launch, and not a factory plan.

## Announced

{{< status-board announced >}}

Cowboy Space, formerly Aetherflux, is in the [company directory](/companies/#aetherflux). Reason-1 launched on 1 October 2026 to demonstrate laser power beaming. That flight is a power demonstration, so it is not a row. Stampede, Big Iron, and Reason-2 are.

SpaceX's AI1 row stays on the 16 September 2026 spacecraft figures. On 10 November 2026 the company ordered a heterojunction solar-cell production line for Bastrop, with first delivery targeted for the first half of 2027. That order is factory equipment. It is not a launch slot, and the row does not move. The 25 November 2026 FCC grant is the authorization row below. It does not put AI1 on a manifest.
