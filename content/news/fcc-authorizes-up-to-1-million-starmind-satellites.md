description: "The FCC Space Bureau granted in part SpaceX's application for up to one million Starmind satellites. The grant is backup Ka-band radio. Six nominal shells run from 550 km to 1,002 km, and satellites that will operate above 600 km need a later debris filing. The January application had asked for altitudes up to 2,000 km."
source: "https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DA-26-1273A1.pdf"
icon: "📡"
bullets:
  - "DA 26-1273, adopted and released 25 November 2026 by Space Bureau Chief Courtney Reinhard, under delegated authority. File SAT-LOA-20260108-00016, call sign S00798. SpaceX filed on 30 January 2026. The Bureau accepted the application for filing on 4 February. The order grants it in part and defers it in part"
  - "The scope is backup and emergency telemetry, tracking, and command for up to 1,000,000 satellites, in 18.8–19.3 GHz space-to-Earth and 28.6–29.1 GHz Earth-to-space, on an unprotected, non-interference basis. Primary communications stay optical links, including to Starlink. SpaceX must finish federal coordination before transmitting in 18.8–19.3 GHz, and it needs earth-station authority before a Starmind satellite talks to a gateway. This is not a launch license"
  - "Six nominal shells: 550–568 km at 26–32°, 565–585 km at 97.7°, 686–718 km at 30°, 707–744 km at 97.2°, 946–978 km at 30°, and 967–1,002 km at 99.4°. The January application asked for 500–2,000 km"
  - "The Bureau found the debris plan compliant for satellites that will operate below 600 km. A satellite that will operate above 600 km needs a modification with a final debris plan before deployment. Four of the six shells are above that line. A request to dispose of satellites above 600 km into Earth or heliocentric orbits, rather than atmospheric reentry within five years, is deferred"
  - "The grant is void if SpaceX misses a date: one satellite in its assigned orbit and operating by 25 November 2033, 10% of the 1,000,000 maximum (100,000) by 25 November 2035, 50% (500,000) by 25 November 2038, and the remaining satellites launched and operating by 25 November 2040. The 15-year term starts at 3 a.m. EST on the day SpaceX certifies the first satellite. The Bureau waived the surety bond and the processing-round rules. These dates do not replace the company's late-2027 flight target, and they do not change the 16 September 2026 spacecraft figures"
  - "Petitions to deny from Viasat, Kuiper Systems (Amazon Leo), and the American Astronomical Society are denied. The Bureau will not require further NEPA review. The FCC news release calls this the first U.S. authorization for a constellation of data centers in space. The Starcloud, Blue Origin, Orbital, and Cowboy Space applications are still filings"

The [news release](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DOC-427572A1.pdf) is the Commission's announcement. The order is the grant. It does not put a Starmind satellite on a manifest. The spacecraft row stays the 16 September 2026 design. This license is the [authorization row](/status/#authorized).
