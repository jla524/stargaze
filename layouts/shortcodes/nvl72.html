Order from Nvidia’s DGX Vera Rubin NVL72 figure. Every tray inserts from this face. Each power shelf is 3U and rated 110 kW, with six air-cooled 18.3 kW supplies, so the four shelves are 440 kW of installed supply. They feed a liquid-cooled 54 V busbar. Two 100 A whips at 415–480 V AC feed each shelf. An AMAX partner sheet lists the rack at 188 kW Max Q and 228 kW Max P. That is an integrator draw, not a SpaceX figure, and it is not the shelf nameplate.

Where the NVLink copper runs

ToR Power Compute Switch Compute Power

The bright path is one GPU link: out the rear of a top compute tray, down the cartridge, into a switch tray. Nvidia’s block diagram shows every Rubin fanning out to all nine switch trays. Descriptions of this spine put it at about 5,000 copper cables, more than two miles, in four serviceable cartridges. Cold plates on the CPUs, GPUs, and networking tie into manifolds in the same rear volume. Facility hoses couple at FD83 fittings. The cartridge is drawn only beside the compute and switch trays.

SemiAnalysis, 11 November 2026: the trays sit back-to-back around the board, “a mini Kyber,” for terrestrial deployment and for orbital deployment. The board is 84 layers of M10 laminate, against 156 layers of M9 the firm cites for the earlier Kyber design. Kyber’s public name has shifted between a single rack and a multi-rack domain, so this page uses it only as that midplane precedent. The strata in the preview are a texture. The layer count is 84. Power shelves, manifolds, and the real tray pitch are absent from the report.

One tray is one operating system and two modules. Each module is one Vera and two Rubin GPUs. Eighteen trays give 36 CPUs and 72 GPUs. GPUDirect reaches GPU memory through the CPU. The tuning guide counts eight ConnectX-9 ASICs on four boards; the DGX user-guide table lists two ConnectX-9 cards. The photo shows two bays. Partner sheets list 288 GB of HBM4 on each Rubin package, 20.7 TB of HBM4 in the rack, and 54 TB of LPDDR5X across the Vera CPUs. NVLink 6 is quoted at 3.6 TB/s per GPU and 260 TB/s across the rack. Those are platform figures, not a Kessel measurement.

Three records, kept apart

RecordWhat it says
24 Aug 2026
Musk and Nvidia		 A space-optimized Vera Rubin NVL72 for launch in the fourth quarter of 2027, with significant scale in 2028. Musk called the design simpler, lower cost, denser, and lighter than a traditional rack.
11 Nov 2026
SemiAnalysis		 Codename Kessel. Same 72 GPU packages, 18 compute trays, and 9 switch trays. Cable cartridges replaced by an 84-layer M10 midplane. Trays rearranged back-to-back around that board, for the ground and for orbit. SpaceX would buy chip packages from Nvidia and procure the rest, skipping Nvidia’s assembled compute boards. The firm did not say this is the August design. SpaceX and Nvidia have not confirmed it.
16 Sep 2026
AI1 spacecraft		 The current public vehicle on the spec sheet: up to 4,000 kg, solar array above 1,500 m², 250 kW peak and 175 kW average, 75 m wingspan, 160 m² of liquid radiators. Those figures describe the spacecraft. Trade descriptions put an assembled Vera Rubin NVL72 near 4,000 lb. Kessel’s mass, draw, and radiator hookup are unpublished.

On the ground, cold plates, an in-rack manifold, and FD83 hoses carry heat to facility water. In orbit, the September 2026 AI1 design quotes 160 m² of liquid radiators for the spacecraft. The Kessel report does not describe the loop between the tray cold plates and those radiators. Radiator area sets how long the heat can be sustained.
